Website Evaluations (CRAP)

I evaluated these sites using contrast, repetition, alignment, and proximity (CRAP) principles.

Apple

  • Contrast: Large product names and short promotional lines establish a clear visual hierarchy, while the prominent product imagery draws attention to each featured item.
  • Repetition: Product sections reuse a consistent pattern of a product heading, brief message, image, and action links such as “Learn more” and “Buy.”
  • Alignment: Each product feature keeps its heading, supporting copy, and actions together in a predictable block, making the long home page easy to scan.
  • Proximity: Each product’s description and action links sit with its related product image, so visitors can quickly tell which item the links refer to.

UNC Charlotte

  • Contrast: The main headline, supporting introduction, and calls to action are given different levels of emphasis, helping visitors find the university’s central message first.
  • Repetition: Program features and news stories repeat a heading, short description, and follow-up link pattern, creating consistency across different content types.
  • Alignment: The navigation groups academics, admissions, campus life, research, athletics, and about links; the page content follows a clear sequence of sections beneath it.
  • Proximity: Each program or story places its descriptive text beside its related link, and the quick links group common student resources together.
